## Goal and review status

Describe what the user should experience before fixing interfaces. The first
proof is welcome, grounded initiative with no assigned work task. Operational
correctness is necessary, but does not establish conversational value.

The original S1–S15 IDs are preserved. S11–S15 are revised, S16–S25 are added,
and S1–S10 remain the work-family specifications for M4. There are 25 scenarios.
The earlier walkthrough and question-resolution checkmarks are superseded by
this revision; the owner approved M1 on 2026-09-17. See
[M1 acceptance](../evidence/M1-acceptance.md).

Each scenario names a situation, observable behavior, and the claim tested.
The walkthrough below records trigger → decision → state transition. State
updates are proposed or derived from inputs and committed by the shell;
the decision core does not directly mutate stored or external state.

## Primary family: proactive conversation

The user has enabled bounded initiative, configured observation scope and
notification preferences, and chatted naturally. No assigned work task is
required. The reference application supports both unsolicited messages and
ordinary replies in the same channel.

### S11 — Reconnecting after an absence

- **Situation:** The user returns after three days; a shared topic is available.
- **Expected:** Consider a concrete, relevant opening grounded in that history.
  Speak only if there is something worth adding; otherwise wait. Presence
  alone does not require a greeting or a forced reference to an old topic.
- **Validates:** Memory-backed continuity and discretionary social timing.

### S12 — A finding during quiet hours

- **Situation:** A useful finding arrives at 2 AM during configured quiet hours.
- **Expected:** Save it and wait until an allowed opportunity. Before delivery,
  recheck freshness, relevance, prior delivery, and current notification rules.
  Discard or close the candidate if it is no longer worth sharing.
- **Validates:** Hard delivery gates and reconsideration of delayed content.

### S13 — Interests change over time

- **Situation:** User-authored discussion or explicit feedback indicates a
  changing interest; other topics have become inactive.
- **Expected:** Update interest evidence and bounded exploration cadence.
  The shell applies any new wake times within the shared budget. AI-generated
  messages do not count as evidence of user interest. Silence alone is weak
  evidence, not proof of dislike. A topic may become inactive or be removed.
- **Validates:** Feedback into perception scheduling without self-reinforcement.

### S14 — Holding back after earlier initiations

- **Situation:** The daily initiation limit is reached and a moderately useful
  item is discovered.
- **Expected:** No additional unsolicited message. Keep it for reconsideration
  or end that candidate. A later budget reset never guarantees delivery.
- **Validates:** Disturbance limits independent of estimated content value.

### S15 — "Don't bother me for a while"

- **Situation:** The user requests temporary quiet.
- **Expected:** Persist a mute for unsolicited conversation and acknowledge its
  duration or resume condition in the direct reply. User-initiated replies
  remain possible and do not automatically clear the mute. Existing bounded
  exploration may continue; a request to stop observation is handled by S25.
  M4 work continues only within its mandate, with unsolicited notifications
  still subject to the mute unless a separate exception was explicitly granted.
- **Validates:** Distinct delivery, observation, and task-cancellation semantics.

### S16 — Initiative without a work assignment

- **Situation:** A scheduled opportunity occurs after earlier conversation
  about a small game; there is no new message or assigned task.
- **Expected:** Identify a specific unresolved question grounded in shared
  context and optionally query the allowed source within the exploration
  budget. If nothing is worth investigating, wait. Do not manufacture work.
- **Validates:** Self-initiated information gathering from shared interests.

### S17 — Sharing a worthwhile discovery

- **Situation:** Exploration produces new information relevant to a shared topic.
- **Expected:** Offer a concrete contribution, connect it naturally to the
  conversation, and retain the supporting source. Claims such as "I found"
  require an actual recorded retrieval. Distinguish a suggestion from a fact.
  Speak only when notification rules also allow it.
- **Validates:** Grounded substance, not merely a well-timed message.

### S18 — Exploration finds nothing worth sharing

- **Situation:** Retrieved material is redundant, weak, or irrelevant.
- **Expected:** Record the outcome and wait or close the exploration silently.
  There is no compulsory "I checked for you" update or unbounded follow-up.
- **Validates:** Investigation value and conversational value are separate.

### S19 — The user takes up the opening

- **Situation:** The user replies to a proactive message, asks about its source,
  or develops the idea further.
- **Expected:** Continue in the same conversational context, with access to the
  actual finding and earlier history. Update topic notes and pause unrelated
  unsolicited openings during the active exchange. Ordinary replies still
  obey resource/tool limits, but do not consume the unsolicited-message quota.
- **Validates:** A bidirectional conversational loop rather than a notification feed.

### S20 — The user does not reply

- **Situation:** An unsolicited opening receives no response.
- **Expected:** Do not send reminders merely to obtain a reply. Record nonresponse
  without declaring disinterest. Enforce a cooldown; any future opening needs
  an independent reason and must pass the normal gates.
- **Validates:** Restraint and conservative interpretation of ambiguous feedback.

### S21 — The user corrects memory or initiative preferences

- **Situation:** The user says "I dropped that game idea" or "less of this kind
  of message, please."
- **Expected:** Update the current fact or preference with source evidence,
  invalidate incompatible queued openings, and adjust later choices. Historical
  notes must not override the correction or recreate the obsolete interest.
- **Validates:** Observable adaptation and temporal consistency of memory.

### S22 — Duplicate information or output triggers

- **Situation:** A finding, wake-up, or result event is delivered twice.
- **Expected:** Deduplicate by stable identity and exact delivery state. A
  rephrasing of the same finding is not sufficient reason to share it again.
  Semantic similarity may flag candidates, but is not an execution identity.
- **Validates:** Event dedupe and prevention of repetitive conversation.

### S23 — Restart during conversation or exploration

- **Situation:** The process stops after saving a result, while waiting, or
  while a chat-send request has an unknown outcome.
- **Expected:** Restore persisted context, mute/cooldown, budgets, pending work,
  and wake conditions. Reconsume pending result events safely. Verify unknown
  external effects or surface them to the host; never blindly resend.
- **Validates:** Recovery with explicit limits on exactly-once guarantees.

### S24 — Exploration resources run out

- **Situation:** The model/exploration budget or deadline is exhausted.
- **Expected:** Stop new model/tool work and record an inspectable exhausted
  state using code. An unassigned interest does not require a farewell message.
  Any status delivery obeys notification rules. Do not repeatedly wake an
  exhausted exploration without a host-authorized reset or budget renewal.
- **Validates:** Bounded initiative and no notification bypass.

### S25 — The user stops exploration

- **Situation:** The user says "stop looking into that" or disables proactive
  exploration entirely.
- **Expected:** Persist the cancellation and remove relevant pending wakes.
  Recheck before dispatching actions. Late results may be recorded but cannot
  restart the cancelled work or generate unsolicited output. Explain that an
  already completed external effect cannot be undone by cancellation.
- **Validates:** Observation control distinct from muting conversation.

## Secondary family: watching a deployment (M4)

These preserved scenarios test generality after the conversational experience
has been validated. Their technical counterparts are scheduled for M2 validation.

### S1 — Accepting a watch request

- **Situation:** The user asks to watch a deployment.
- **Expected:** Create a bounded task with objective, success condition, scope,
  budget, expiry, and initial wake time; acknowledge and start monitoring.
- **Validates:** Explicit work mandate and lifecycle creation.

### S2 — Silence when nothing is wrong

- **Situation:** A check finds normal operation.
- **Expected:** Record the observation, schedule the next check, and stay silent.
- **Validates:** Continued work without compulsory user-facing output.

### S3 — An error signal appears

- **Situation:** An ambiguous error appears in the logs.
- **Expected:** Compare investigation, notification, and waiting; investigate
  relevant changes or dependencies before notifying on this ambiguous evidence.
  The result returns to the same task by explicit identity.
- **Validates:** Active information gathering and outcome feedback.

### S4 — Insufficient evidence

- **Situation:** Investigation leaves the situation ambiguous.
- **Expected:** Persist a wait until two minutes later or a relevant new error.
- **Validates:** Time- and event-based continuation.

### S5 — Notification with evidence

- **Situation:** The anomaly persists and evidence warrants intervention.
- **Expected:** Notify once with useful evidence when permitted; persist exact
  delivery state. If delivery is blocked, hold and reconsider it later.
- **Validates:** Auditable intervention under notification constraints.

### S6 — Repeated identical anomalies

- **Situation:** The same incident keeps producing duplicate signals.
- **Expected:** No repeated notification of unchanged information. A distinct
  incident or meaningful escalation requires a new, justified decision.
- **Validates:** Precise incident/delivery state rather than semantic guessing.

### S7 — Recovery

- **Situation:** The deployment satisfies the success condition.
- **Expected:** Complete this watch and cancel its pending checks. Late results
  cannot reopen it; any future watch needs a separate authorized lifecycle.
- **Validates:** Clean completion.

### S8 — Budget exhaustion

- **Situation:** Resources run out while a promised watch remains unresolved.
- **Expected:** Persist an exhausted terminal state and an outstanding status
  report. Use a host-reserved reporting allowance if configured; otherwise
  expose the status through the host. Deliver under allowed notification rules
  without spending the exhausted work budget or bypassing mute.
- **Validates:** Honest commitment status and independent delivery constraints.

### S9 — Process restart

- **Situation:** The host restarts mid-watch.
- **Expected:** Restore tasks, waits, budgets, and actions. Known completed work
  is not repeated; unknown effects are verified or handed to the host.
- **Validates:** The same recovery contract as S23 in a work setting.

### S10 — Curiosity finds an unrelated signal

- **Situation:** Investigation finds an interesting but unrelated signal.
- **Expected:** Optionally propose a separate bounded exploration under the
  existing interest grant, or refrain. Do not create a new work mandate, widen
  tool scope, or consume the task's reserved resources.
- **Validates:** Origination limits and separation of work/exploration budgets.

## Decision walkthrough

These are working semantics, not finalized API signatures. Input handling can
propose local state changes; the shell validates and persists them alongside
any decision. User preference commands and recovery bookkeeping do not require
an LLM call or a world-facing tool action.

## Open questions and provisional choices

1. **Origination (S10/S16):** Within an enabled exploration grant, topic-linked
   explorations may start automatically under small per-exploration and shared
   budgets, an active-count cap, and expiry. Work mandates require user/host
   authorization. M2 must record the actual limits before implementation;
   M3 reviews whether the behavior is welcome. A topic mention alone is not
   authorization to widen observation scope.
2. **Weak signals (S13):** M2 starts with bounded aggregation of user-authored
   evidence and explicit feedback. Thresholds and cadence are prototype choices;
   no generic Attention interface is required. M3 evaluates missed opportunities.
3. **Mute duration (S15):** Explicit duration wins. Otherwise use a documented
   host default communicated in the direct acknowledgement; without one, mute
   until explicit resume. Exploration cancellation is a separate control.
4. **Channel, source, and default limits:** Select and document in M2 before
   live use. Require a channel that can deliver unsolicited messages and receive
   replies, and a queryable source with recorded provenance. Start conservatively;
   configured quotas are ceilings, not targets to fill.
5. **Memory retrieval:** Start with bounded background, recent records, and
   topic-linked notes. Semantic indexing is deferred until M3 evidence shows a
   concrete retrieval failure that simpler selection cannot adequately address.
6. **Experience acceptance:** M3 defines the trial protocol and rubric before
   the trial. Owner judgement, failure cases, and cost are required; no reply-rate
   target or architecture-correctness test can approve the experience alone.

## Architecture strain log

Entries preserve the original conflicts and record revised dispositions.
A documented resolution is not a claim that implementation or validation is done.

1. **Outcome → event path (original S3; now also S16–S18/S23).** The old diagram
   omitted result routing. **Design resolution:** persist action-to-follow-up
   identity; commit outcome and pending event together; consume recoverably.
   Terminal/cancelled work stays closed on late outcomes. Implement and test in M2.
   **M2b implementation follow-through (2026-09-18):** query outcomes now carry
   action/exploration IDs and commit with pending result events; terminal sends
   remain acknowledgements. Replays cover recovery, duplicates and late results.
   Delivery mute is separated from research authority as required by entry 7.
   See [M2b evidence](../evidence/M2b-initiative.md); live model acceptance remains
   pending. This implements the accepted resolution rather than revising it.
2. **Exhaustion notice bypass (original S8).** The earlier proposed exception
   conflicted with hard disturbance gates. **Superseded:** stop and persist status
   without a model call; a promised task gets an outstanding report using reserved
   resources/host status and permitted delivery. No blanket mute bypass. M2 tests
   S24; M4 tests the commitment-reporting behavior in S8.
3. **Decision → perception feedback (original S13).** Interest changes need to
   affect later observation. **Design resolution:** the shell validates and applies
   cadence updates from stored user evidence/preferences within shared limits.
   Source polling, evaluation cadence, and notification timing are distinct.
   Implement the minimal feedback in M2; assess its quality in M3.
4. **First proof misaligned with product intent (2026-09-17).** Work-first M2
   could succeed without proving worthwhile spontaneous conversation.
   **Revision:** M2 builds conversation, M3 validates experience, M4 adds work.
5. **Premature abstraction and content responsibility.** The previous plan
   extracted the core before validating initiative quality and treated draft
   quality solely as the host's concern. **Revision:** one decider is sufficient
   initially; end-to-end content quality is accepted in M2/M3; extraction is M4.
6. **Topic, concern, and memory conflation.** Semantic similarity does not imply
   one lifecycle or an execution identity. **Resolution:** topics organize
   knowledge, follow-ups track ongoing attention, and action IDs route outcomes.
   Minimal memory is in M2; a general memory platform is outside MVP scope.
7. **Mute was underspecified (S15).** Earlier prose silenced conversation while
   the walkthrough disabled all interest evaluations. **Resolution:** delivery
   mute and observation cancellation are separate controls, with visible resume
   conditions. Direct user replies do not silently unmute proactive output.

8. **Execution evidence gap (2026-09-17).** Clear prose still allowed unchecked
   phase transitions, self-certified owner reviews, and fixtures presented as
   live behavior. **Resolution:** stable acceptance IDs, required evidence kinds,
   a shared evidence register, per-milestone handoffs, and a static consistency
   checker. These enforce inspectable claims; they cannot authenticate evidence
   or guarantee another assistant follows the process. All acceptance remains
   pending until the actual required evidence exists.

9. **Ambiguous M1 reviewer (2026-09-17).** E2–E4 said "reviewed" but required
   only document evidence. **Resolution:** require both documented material and
   owner acceptance for all three; the owner may accept them in one scoped
   review. Existing empty evidence and pending acceptance remain unchanged.
